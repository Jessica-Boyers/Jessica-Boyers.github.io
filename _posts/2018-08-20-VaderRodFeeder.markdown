description: |
  **Context:** As my Master of Engineering thesis at RIT, in 2018-2019 I took on the design project leadership and project management role of a team of 5 graduate students performing an industry-sponsored R&D project.
  
  **Motivation:**  The VADER (since acquired by Xerox) metal additive machine uses magneto-hydrodynamic jetting to "3D Print" metal from wire spools. Using rods could save significant cost, improve material abailability and secure supply chain, and allow for new alloys which cannot be drawn into wire to be used in printing.
  
  **Objective:** Design and demonstrate a rod feeding concept for metal AM machines that can:
  - Store at least thirty 1ft. rods of 1/8" dia.
  - Maintain continuous, gap-less supply.
  - Feed fast enough to keep up with demand.
  - Be controlled by digital signal from AM machine.

  **Design:** In addition to my role as leader and PM, I was a technical contributor to mechanical and electrical design. I had ownership of budget and schedule ($2500, 9 month timeline), directing the activities of team members and managing the critical path, alignment of requirements with sponsor, and study design. We took an iterative design approach emphasizing rapid prototyping and Design Of Experiments to focus in on the optimal concept. 
  
  The final architecture uses an operator-loaded hopper (loaded at shift start). On command from the AM machine, an agitation and metering mechanism transitions rods from bulk storage into a controlled feeder that projects rods into the crucible at the required rate with no gap between rods.
  
  **Outcome:** Delivered a working prototype **on time and under budget**, verified conformance to requirements in formal test execution, validated core functionality in a live demonstration (ImagineRIT), and met sponsor expectations for feasibility and usability.
